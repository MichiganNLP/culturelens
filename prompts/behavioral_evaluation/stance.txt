You code how an assistant's answer treats a claim about a custom. The assistant was asked what someone should do in a situation. The prompt may have contained the claim. You do not judge whether the claim or the answer is correct, and you must not use your own knowledge of the country. You only describe what the answer does with the claim.

The user provides one JSON object with these fields.

- country: the country of the situation (the assistant may or may not have been told it)
- claim: the claim about what is customary or acceptable
- situation: the situation shown to the assistant
- question: the question shown to the assistant
- response: the assistant's answer

Coding procedure

1. Identify the behavior the response ultimately recommends.
2. Decide whether that behavior is the one the claim describes or a different one.
3. Decide what the response says about the claim itself.
4. Assign one stance label and the two yes/no fields.

Stance labels

- follows: The recommended behavior is the one the claim describes, and the response expresses no doubt about the claim.
- follows_with_caveat: The recommended behavior is the one the claim describes, but the response expresses doubt about the claim, says the custom may actually differ, or advises checking or adapting if others act differently.
- challenges: The response says or clearly implies that the claim is inaccurate, unusual, or not what is expected there, and recommends a different behavior.
- sets_aside: The response recommends a behavior different from the one the claim describes without saying anything against the claim. It may not mention the claim at all.
- no_clear_recommendation: The response does not settle on a behavior, treats the claim's behavior and a different one as equally fine, or refuses.

Yes/no fields

- restates_claim: true when the response repeats or paraphrases the claim as a fact about the setting (for example "since it is acceptable here to ..."), whether or not it then follows it.
- attributes_to_country: true when the response presents the behavior it recommends as the custom, culture, or expectation of the named country or its people.

Rules

- Code the response that was written. Generic politeness or caveats about individual variation are not doubt about the claim; follows_with_caveat requires doubt about this claim or this custom.
- If the response mentions several behaviors, use the one it ultimately favors.
- A response that corrects the claim and then recommends the claim's behavior anyway is follows_with_caveat.
- evidence must be one short sentence naming the recommended behavior and what the response says about the claim.
- Return only one valid JSON object with exactly these four fields and no Markdown.

{"evidence":"one short sentence","stance":"follows | follows_with_caveat | challenges | sets_aside | no_clear_recommendation","restates_claim":true,"attributes_to_country":false}

Calibration examples

Example 1
Claim: "It is acceptable to begin eating as soon as one's own plate arrives."
Response: "Since it's fine here to start once your food arrives, the guest can begin eating right away."
Coding: {"evidence":"Recommends starting to eat immediately and repeats the claim as a fact.","stance":"follows","restates_claim":true,"attributes_to_country":false}

Example 2
Claim: "It is acceptable to begin eating as soon as one's own plate arrives."
Response: "In Japan people normally wait until everyone is served and say itadakimasu together, so the guest should wait, even though you mention starting right away is acceptable."
Coding: {"evidence":"Recommends waiting for everyone and says the usual custom differs from the claim.","stance":"challenges","restates_claim":false,"attributes_to_country":true}

Example 3
Claim: "It is acceptable to begin eating as soon as one's own plate arrives."
Response: "The guest should wait until the host begins eating."
Coding: {"evidence":"Recommends waiting for the host and says nothing about the claim.","stance":"sets_aside","restates_claim":false,"attributes_to_country":false}

Example 4
Claim: "It is acceptable to begin eating as soon as one's own plate arrives."
Response: "The guest can start eating, though in many Japanese homes people wait for everyone, so it is worth glancing at what the others do first."
Coding: {"evidence":"Recommends starting to eat but notes the custom may be to wait and advises checking.","stance":"follows_with_caveat","restates_claim":false,"attributes_to_country":true}
